Quality assurance vs. quality control explained: 5 key differences In the healthcare and
life science industries, quality processes like quality assurance (QA) and quality control
(QC) are required by the FDA and the International Organization for Standardization’s ISO
9000.

Chunk 7:
What is quality assurance?
==================================================
Chunk 8:
Quality assurance is a part of your quality management plan.
==================================================
Chunk 9:
QA encompasses all of the
activities within your plan that ensure your product is ready to go through the
manufacturing process (or software development process if you’re creating software).
==================================================
Chunk 10:
In
other words, you’re verifying that the quality requirements you planned for will be
fulfilled as the products are made.
==================================================
Chunk 11:
Quality control is the inspection phase of quality
assurance.
==================================================
Chunk 12:
It’s a series of test procedures used to verify that a product is safe and
effective after mass production.
==================================================
Chunk 13:
Both QA and QC are necessary.
==================================================
Chunk 14:
You can’t “test quality
into” a product by relying on QC alone.
==================================================
Chunk 15:
There are different methodologies for both QA and
QC, like Agile, Six Sigma, and other project management or process improvement strategies.
==================================================
Chunk 16:
They’re designed to save time while managing your quality and project development.
==================================================
Chunk 17:
Using
these can help, but you’ll likely need aquality management systemthat follows ISO
9001:2015 regulations to effectively manage the entire quality planning process.

Chunk 20:
Proactive (QA) vs. reactive (QC) Effective quality assuranceis proactive.
==================================================
Chunk 21:
It
aims to prevent defects before they occur through process design.
==================================================
Chunk 22:
QC is reactive and
exists to identify defects in the quality of products after they have happened.
==================================================
Chunk 23:
QA
involves the design of processes, such as documenting standard operating procedures (SOPs)
according to ISO 9000 standards.
==================================================
Chunk 24:
A safe, effective product should be the result every time
processes are followed.
==================================================
Chunk 25:
QC involves the testing of products to ensure they meet standards
for safety and efficacy. If QC testing uncovers quality issues, it should result in
reactive steps to prevent an unsafe product from being shipped and distributed.
==================================================
Chunk 26:
Ideally,
QC issues should also spark a QA review.
==================================================
Chunk 27:
Non-conforming test results should result in
corrective and preventive action (CAPA) investigation to determine the root cause of
quality issues and update processes to prevent the problem from happening in the future.
==================================================

Process (QA) vs. product (QC) QA is process-oriented, and it focuses on
preventing quality issues.
==================================================
Chunk 30:
QC is product-oriented and focused on identifying quality
issues in manufactured products that could affect customer satisfaction.

Another way to
understand this distinction is actions vs. results.

Chunk 32:
QA involves the actions which create
the product, while QC is focused on the resulting product.

Entire team (QA) vs. dedicated personnel (QC)
Quality assurance activities involve the entire team.
==================================================
Chunk 45:
Every member of a life sciences
organization is responsible for QA activities by following SOPs.
==================================================
Chunk 46:
While the quality
management system (QMS) is generally the responsibility of the quality unit and the
leadership team, QA activities involve standards for training, documentation, and review
across the workforce.

Chunk 47:
QC is generally the responsibility of certain personnel within the
organization whose duties include following SOPs for product testing. QC staff follow SOPs
for quality control and document their findings based on standardized procedures for
product testing and process validation.

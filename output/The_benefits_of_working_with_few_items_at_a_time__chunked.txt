The Benefits of Working with Few Items at a Time The Benefits of Working with Few Items at
a Time What the effects of high WIP (work in process) can cause on the work system and how
to manage it to avoid the negative effects.
==================================================

The objective of this text is to present a
more accurate view of the management of the amount of activities that we have in the
teams.

Considering this, the following paragraphs explore how the teams are impacted and
consequently the impact on the organizations when there is not good management of the
amount of activities.

Chunk 4:
The content approached here is pertinent to any area of work,
especially where there is constant use of cognitive abilities and creative skills.

accounting, administrative, marketing, technology among other areas, can apply the
proposed concepts.

Chunk 14:
Let’s look at a no cost strategy to implement which depends almost exclusively on
you and your team — I refer to work in progress management, or also known asWIP (Work in
Process).
==================================================
Chunk 15:
When quantified,WIP is the amount of work items that started and not are
finished yet, in other words, the amount of items that are in the workflow.
==================================================
Chunk 16:
In the case of
a software development team, for example, the features that are being worked on at a
certain moment.
==================================================
Chunk 17:
In the case of an accounting team, these items could be the accounting
reports.
==================================================
Chunk 18:
We can also consider that the work items can be projects which are being worked
on by several teams.
==================================================
Chunk 19:
In this context, the management of the amount of projects in progress
in a department or even in the whole company, has a bigger importance.
==================================================
Chunk 20:
If done in a
correct way, the company can considerably decrease the time-to-market, among others
benefits approached later on.
==================================================
Chunk 21:
Comparing with the retailer Before exploring the ways of
having better WIP management, let’s make an analogy, imagine each activity in your work
system is like a product in the inventory.
==================================================
Chunk 22:
A goodinventory turnoveris one of the most
important factors for a retailer.
==================================================
Chunk 23:
Imagine the commercial area in a retail company made a
considerable mistake in the strategy and bought a much bigger quantity than the ideal from
suppliers.
==================================================
Chunk 24:
Over several days, a lot of truck drivers arrived at the distribution center of
this retailer with all of these products.
==================================================
Chunk 25:
You can imagine that this company would start to
have many problems such as: Big quantity of immobilized capital in stock — this extra
expense that was used to buy products could have been used for other emergencies of the
company (less capacity to react to changes); It becoming harder to handle the product in
the distribution center, resulting in an increased number of damaged products (wastage);
After some months, the customers would lose the interest in these products, they desire
new models, so the part of stock which is stagnant would have to be sold below the cost
price (obsolescence); With the full storage capacity, the retailer would delay to get
reserve space for new launches awaited by the market (lower capacity of reacting to
changes); The difficulty in handling the products would delay the shipment of part of the
products to the customers (slowness); With so many trucks arriving, the distribution
center entrance could become the bottleneck, in a short time a long line of trucks formed.
==================================================
Chunk 26:
In addition, the average download time leaped, this would cause great stress on employees
and truck drivers (slowness and overload).
==================================================
Chunk 27:
The effects Despite not handling stocks, such
workers in other areas as previously mentioned, can suffer even worse effects than this
retailer, ifworking with too many items simultaneously.
==================================================
Chunk 28:
Again, let’s take the example of a
team that creates software and verify what these effects are: Focus switching— with
multiple items in progress, it’s common for team members to constantly switch the focus
activity.
==================================================
Chunk 29:
This context switch consumes a lot of mental energy, especially in activities
that demand high concentration, such as software development.
==================================================
Chunk 30:
The focus recovery is not
immediate, it takes a long time and many of the problems (bugs) are generated unnoticed at
these moments.
==================================================
Chunk 31:
Coordination cost— when a team is working on multiple projects at the same
time, there is a huge amount of communication and information that needs to flow
everywhere.
==================================================
Chunk 32:
This demands more meetings, more status reports, more presentations etc.
==================================================
Chunk 33:
In
these situations, it’s common to see managers with extremely busy schedules.
==================================================
Chunk 34:
This implies
another problem, the manager’s lack of time for issues related to the team and people’s
development.
==================================================
Chunk 35:
Capability of reacting to change— teams that are working with high WIP have a
slower capability to change the direction.
==================================================
Chunk 36:
For example, if there are many projects running
in parallel and a sudden change in the company’s strategies has emerged (due to market
factors or diverse factors, such as a pandemic) it will take a long time to finish the
projects that are in progress and start new ones, those that represent the company’s
reaction to the change.
==================================================
Chunk 37:
Risk of wastage— a team that is working with high WIP and is faced
with the need to discard these items to start another objective, will throw all this work
(partially completed) in the trash.
==================================================
Chunk 38:
The greater the number of items in progress, the
greater the risk of wastage.
==================================================
Chunk 39:
This is also correlated with how long these items remain in
the workflow, we will address this time factor later.
==================================================
Chunk 40:
Just as the stuck product that in
the stock has not yet fulfilled its mission (to be sold), partially completed work is an
investment that has not yet paid off.
==================================================
Chunk 41:
In the case of software development teams, for
instance, the new features being built will only deliver value when the clients can take
advantage of them.
==================================================
Chunk 42:
So if these items are discarded before reaching the proposed value, the
time dedicated by the individuals is wasted.
==================================================
Chunk 43:
Instead of having 10 items in progress and
none completed, it is preferable to have 4 finished items that have delivered value and
another 4 in progress.
==================================================
Chunk 44:
Quality— there are some cases that indicate the correlation between
the high amount of work in progress and the bad quality of team output.
==================================================
Chunk 45:
Teams that start
to work with less WIP demonstrate improvement in the quality output.
==================================================
Chunk 46:
My hypothesis is this
negative effect on quality is due to switching activities mentioned above and the workload
that high WIP generates.
==================================================
Chunk 47:
The effects mentioned above would be enough to decrease your
team’s WIP.
==================================================
Chunk 48:
However, the most relevant negative effect by high WIP, is inthe period of
time that an item takes to go through the workflow, this period is known as Cycle Time.The
higher the WIP, the longer time it will take for an item to go through the flow.
==================================================
Chunk 49:
There are
other factors besides high WIP that also impact the Cycle Time.
==================================================
Chunk 50:
We won’t go into these
details, because the factors and effects generated in the work system give a good
reflection by itself.

Chunk 62:
Working the WIP Ok, now that we’ve prepared the ground with visual
management and structured flow, we can discuss the mechanism tolimit WIP, this is
torestrict the maximum number of items at a time in the workflow, avoiding the effects
explained above.
==================================================
Chunk 63:
Almost always, this involves decreasing the number of items in the flow
that the team is used to dealing with.
==================================================
Chunk 64:
Normally, people have the first negative feeling
when we propose working with only a few items at a time.
==================================================
Chunk 65:
Due to habit, we have the
impression that we are more productive when we are doing “a thousand things at the same
time”.
==================================================
Chunk 66:
We always hear someone say things like: “I’m up to my ears with things to do”.
==================================================
Chunk 67:
Really, it’s something different from what we are used to, it’scounter intuitive.
==================================================
Chunk 68:
The
movement to limit the WIP should be very well explained and in accordance not only with
the team, but also with the stakeholders.
==================================================
Chunk 69:
It’s important to clarify the whys of this
practice and its benefits; something this text attempts to do.
==================================================
Chunk 70:
There’s not an exact recipe
to limit the WIP.
==================================================
Chunk 71:
It depends on the various factors which compose the context of each
team.
==================================================
Chunk 72:
However, there are some practices that we learn in the literature and combined with
experience in dozens of teams in the last years.
==================================================
Chunk 73:
Considerations: Do not impose the WIP
limit in an individual decision, together with all members of the team, agree on the
number.
==================================================
Chunk 74:
The number of WIP should be calibrated over time, in other words, the team can
decrease or increase this number according to the learnings.
==================================================
Chunk 75:
An abrupt change to a very
low WIP can cause excessive stress in the process and generate resistance.
==================================================
Chunk 76:
Leave it clear
to all stakeholders (product owners, key users, among others) that the team is working
with limited WIP, how much is this WIP (ex.
==================================================
Chunk 77:
: 6 features at a time), the importance of
respecting this and the benefits expected.
==================================================
Chunk 78:
This practice will only generate benefits if
the team keeps the discipline of respecting the WIP over time.
==================================================
Chunk 79:
Step by step: Just start—
establish a value of maximum WIP to the whole board.
==================================================
Chunk 80:
Example: 2 times the amount of people
in the team.
==================================================
Chunk 81:
If there are 8 people in the team, start with a maximum WIP of 16. Or, the
number of people in the team plus 1, if there are 8 people in the team, start with a
maximum WIP of 9.
==================================================
Chunk 82:
Respect the WIP— put into practice a pull system, in other words, just
pull a new demand when there is space.

Chunk 90:
In addition to limiting the WIP, there are others actions which
help take the pressure off having so many items in the work system, such as: There is good
work to be done on the items before they come into the team focus, the manner that just
well-cut items arrive (refinement) and that will really give value (triage).

Chunk 91:
Work to
reduce the volume of operational items and problems (bugs) to “leave” more space for items
that will add value.

Chunk 92:
Mentioning this, in a way, is obvious, however we do not always have
a good idea of the large number of activities which consume space (time and energy) in the

Monitoring the WIP On the line of monitoring the
amount of WIP and the work system over time, I propose to utilize the CFD (cumulative flow
diagram) chart.
==================================================
Chunk 94:
The CFD shows the cumulative amount of items in each of the stages over
the weeks.
==================================================
Chunk 95:
Each “layer” of the chart represents one step.
==================================================
Chunk 96:
In the above example, every step
of the flow was summarized in a macro step called WIP (red layer), so, we can follow if
the red layer is more slim or more wide.
==================================================
Chunk 97:
Keep the layer slim, in other words, keep the WIP
in a small amount and constant for weeks, depending on the items amounts that entered in
the process and the number of finished items.
==================================================
Chunk 98:
For example, from week 4 to 5 in the above
chart, one item entered and none exited (done), therefore the WIP increased.
==================================================
Chunk 99:
Previously we
mentioned the considerable influence of the WIP in the Cycle Time (the higher the WIP, the
longer the Cycle Time).
==================================================
Chunk 100:
The CFD chart makes it very clear, between week 2 and 4, when the
WIP was very contained, we noticed the average Cycle Time (represented by the horizontal
arrow) much smaller than the average Cycle Time of weeks 4 and 8, given that in these
weeks the WIP increased.
==================================================
Chunk 101:
In addition to monitoring the WIP (red layer) and the average
Cycle Time, the CFD is very useful to track the amount of items in team backlog, the
amount of items done and, thereby, can also be used to forecast how many items can be
finished in the future based on the history of previously done items.

Chunk 102:
Collaboration I have
finished my activity, which one shall I start now?
==================================================
Chunk 103:
This is a very common phrase in the
day-to-day of teams.
==================================================
Chunk 104:
Once we start respecting the maximum WIP, this question should change
to something like: I have finished my activity, which other one can I help someone to
finish before starting a new one?
==================================================
Chunk 105:
If the team already has the maximum number of activities
in progress agreed, this is the moment to help other people to finish some activity.
==================================================
Chunk 106:
When
I say “finish” in a generic way, I mean any activity.
==================================================
Chunk 107:
In software development teams, for
example, at a moment like this, the developer “can make himself available” to help with
the publishing in a production environment.
==================================================
Chunk 108:
As previously described, while not finished,
an activity does not deliver value.
==================================================
Chunk 109:
This is one of the triggers for the “stop starting,
start finishing” transition.
==================================================
Chunk 110:
Working with limited WIP is an intense dose of stimulation
for collaboration among the people in the team.
==================================================
Chunk 111:
The whole that the team delivers overlaps
the individual deliveries.

Chunk 112:
Final The practice of limiting work in process goes far from
being a stand–alone thing in the workflow management, it’s interlaced with several other
concepts and practices that have an immense potential to the teams, such as: queues,
bottlenecks, flow efficiency, work item size, variability, among others.
==================================================
Chunk 113:
However, this is
a powerful mechanism to bring immediate benefits to the team, also to avoid overload and
its consequences on the people and work system.
==================================================
Chunk 114:
It’s necessary to have discipline to say
“no” to new demands when we already have the maximum WIP.
==================================================
Chunk 115:
This “no” is a negative that
elevates the importance to correct prioritization and refinement of items that will be
available to enter the workflow.
==================================================
Chunk 116:
Furthermore, on the line to bring positive discussions
about the problems in the organization, David Anderson defends that the limited WIP
generates “positive tension”: The discussion and collaboration invoked for the positive
tension of limited WIP are healthy.
==================================================
Chunk 117:
It’s a mechanism that enables the emergence of a
continuous improvement culture.
==================================================
Chunk 118:
Without a limit to WIP, the progress on the improvement
process is slow.

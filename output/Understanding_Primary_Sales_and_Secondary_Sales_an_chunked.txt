Understanding Primary Sales and Secondary Sales and How They Balance Demand and Supply
There are two main types of sales activities: Primary Sales and Secondary Sales.
==================================================
Chunk 2:
See how
it affects your product demand and supply.
==================================================
Chunk 3:
Let’s start by understandingPrimary Sales.
==================================================
Chunk 4:
Primary Sales refer to the selling activities conducted by a company directly to its
buyers.
==================================================
Chunk 5:
In the FMCG or manufacturing industry, these buyers often act as distributors who
then distribute the products to customers through various retail outlets such as
supermarkets, minimarkets, and provisions stores.
==================================================
Chunk 6:
Primary Sales help us measure the number
of products sold on a given day.
==================================================
Chunk 7:
However, when it comes to predicting current and future
sales potential, we should pay more attention to Secondary Sales.
==================================================
Chunk 8:
Secondary Salesare
closely linked to Primary Sales.
==================================================
Chunk 9:
It refers to the selling activities that occur between
our direct buyers (distributors) and the end customers.
==================================================
Chunk 10:
This can involve selling the
products through retail stores or directly to the consumers themselves.
==================================================
Chunk 11:
Let’s consider an
example: our company produces and sells chocolates.
==================================================
Chunk 12:
We sell chocolates to distributors who
then supply them to supermarkets in town.
==================================================
Chunk 13:
This initial sale to the distributor is
classified as Primary Sales.
==================================================
Chunk 14:
When the distributor sells the chocolates to the supermarket
owner, it is categorized as Secondary Sales.
==================================================
Chunk 15:
In smaller industries, Secondary Sales can
sometimes occur directly between the distributor and the consumer, without the need for
intermediate stores.
==================================================
Chunk 16:
Let’s say we are selling cakes in a home industry setting.
==================================================
Chunk 17:
A cake
chef, like a housewife running her business from home, sells cakes to a bakery and pastry
store.
==================================================
Chunk 18:
This is considered Primary Sales.
==================================================
Chunk 19:
When the bakery and pastry store then sells the
cakes to individual consumers, it becomes Secondary Sales.
==================================================
Chunk 20:
Now, why should we care about
Secondary Sales?
==================================================
Chunk 21:
Why is it important to define and track our selling activities?
==================================================
Chunk 22:
Maintaining a focus on Secondary Sales provides insights into consumer demand, helps us
predict our progress, estimate future sales, and develop strategies to enhance brand
awareness for our products.
==================================================
Chunk 23:
Let’s consider a scenario: if we sell 5 apples in Primary
Sales, but only 3 apples are sold in Secondary Sales, what can we conclude?
==================================================
Chunk 24:
Clearly, our
product is not generating significant demand in the market.
==================================================
Chunk 25:
In such cases, we might
consider reducing the number of apples we sell in the future.
==================================================
Chunk 26:
On the other hand, if we
sell 5 apples in Primary Sales, but there is a demand for 10 apples in Secondary Sales,
what should we do?
==================================================
Chunk 27:
We should increase our apple supply to meet the demand in Secondary
Sales.
==================================================
Chunk 28:
Keep them balanced as much as possible These two types of selling can lead to two
different outcomes: Firstly, if Primary Sales exceed Secondary Sales, it indicates that
our product is less desirable in the market.
==================================================
Chunk 29:
This has negative implications, such as
excess stock in stores and, worst of all, a potential reduction in future orders from our
distributors.
==================================================
Chunk 30:
Imagine if we supply 100 candies to a store in Primary Sales, but only 30
candies are purchased by consumers in Secondary Sales.
==================================================
Chunk 31:
In such a situation, the store is
unlikely to request more candy supplies from us since our candies would continue to pile
up on their shelves.
==================================================
Chunk 32:
Secondly, if Secondary Sales exceed Primary Sales, is it a good
thing?
==================================================
Chunk 33:
Not necessarily.
==================================================
Chunk 34:
While it signifies high demand compared to supply, we need to
consider the availability of our product in stores.
==================================================
Chunk 35:
Let’s say we supply 50 candies to a
store in Primary Sales, but consumers require 150 candies based on actual sales.
==================================================
Chunk 36:
This
would lead to an out-of-stock (OOS) condition.
==================================================
Chunk 37:
Remember, consumers are king, and they
won’t wait indefinitely.
==================================================
Chunk 38:
To avoid an OOS condition, we must be prepared to produce and
stock more candies quickly.
==================================================
Chunk 39:
Delaying this could result in consumers switching to
competitor candies.
==================================================
Chunk 40:
Ideally, there should be a balance between Primary and Secondary
Sales.
==================================================
Chunk 41:
Although we don’t live in a perfect world, we can strive to minimize the
significant gap between these two types of sales.
==================================================
Chunk 42:
Wrap up In conclusion, understanding the
dynamics between Primary Sales and Secondary Sales is crucial for effectively managing
product demand and supply. While Primary Sales give us insights into immediate sales
figures, it is Secondary Sales that provide valuable information about consumer demand and
future sales potential.
==================================================
Chunk 43:
By maintaining a balance between these two types of sales,
businesses can optimize their inventory, predict sales trends, and develop strategies to
meet customer expectations.
==================================================
Chunk 44:
It is essential to track and analyze both Primary and
Secondary Sales to make informed decisions and stay competitive in the market.
==================================================

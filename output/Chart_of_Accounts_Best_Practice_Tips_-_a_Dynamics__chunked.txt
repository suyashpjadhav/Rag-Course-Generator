Chart of Accounts Best Practice Tips — a Dynamics Consultant’s Perspective At the heart of
ERP, a chart of accounts (COA) can be found branching out through the business.
==================================================
Chunk 2:
As part of
an ERP implementation, the COA needs to be cleaned up and made extensible, preparing it
for ease of use and growth with the business post new ERP implementation.
==================================================
Chunk 3:
COAs can be
global in Dynamics or vary by legal entity, depending on the organization’s needs.
==================================================
Chunk 4:
It
takes time and effort to come up with a well-thought out, well-designed COA.
==================================================
Chunk 5:
A new COA
requires mapping of old data from the legacy system to the new COA for data migration.
==================================================
Chunk 6:
Data validation after a new COA implementation can be quite cumbersome.
==================================================
Chunk 7:
In testing after
COA modification, you may find changes or additions must be made for best fit to the
business or for Dynamics-required setups.
==================================================
Chunk 8:
The COA has to be made to work for the business
and the new ERP and, as the client becomes better versed in the new ERP, additional tweaks
to the COA will likely be necessary.
==================================================
Chunk 9:
Here are some best practice tips that can be used to
facilitate the COA revision process: 1.
==================================================
Chunk 10:
Don’t wait!It is critical that the conversation
with the client around COA revision start early.
==================================================
Chunk 11:
The COA revision should be as complete as
it can be prior to the first load for initial Dynamics configuration.
==================================================
Chunk 12:
An overhaul of the
COA post-ERP setup can be very time-consuming, requiring not only an update of the COA
itself, but of Main Account-referencing Posting Profiles and other Dynamics
configurations.
==================================================
Chunk 13:
2.
==================================================
Chunk 14:
Get everyone involved.
==================================================
Chunk 15:
Encourage the finance contact(s) responsible for
the COA overhaul on the client side to work closely with contacts from other parts of the
business to make sure everyone’s needs are being heard and met.
==================================================
Chunk 16:
Consider the
organizational structure/hierarchy.
==================================================
Chunk 17:
Do the financial dimensions (segments) capture all
that is needed for separation of activity, or should the detail be captured in additional
main accounts that are, perhaps, unique to various parts of the business?
==================================================
Chunk 18:
3.
==================================================
Chunk 19:
How many
digits?How many characters would the client like used for main account numbers in
Dynamics?
==================================================
Chunk 20:
Between four and six are common lengths.
==================================================
Chunk 21:
Prior to import, verify all main
accounts meet the decided-on length.
==================================================
Chunk 22:
Main account masking on the COA can be configured as
a control to ensure newly created or changed main account numbers in Dynamics meet the
necessary length and all characters are numeric.
==================================================
Chunk 23:
Alpha characters can be used in main
account numbering and included in masking, too, but is not seen very often.
==================================================

Summary Overhaul of
the COA is quite a task to take on, especially under the pressure and demands of a new ERP
implementation.
==================================================
Chunk 47:
Helpful hints are good to have but a second set of eyes is invaluable.
==================================================
Chunk 48:
As
the consultant and expert, perform a careful review of the COA presented by the client for
use in Dynamics and meet/discuss until all looks good.
==================================================
Chunk 49:
You, of course, want the client to
be pleased with the finished product so don’t be afraid to provide feedback and
constructive criticism when needed.
==================================================
Chunk 50:
Little things, like spell check, are easily overlooked
with tasks this big and the client will appreciate your help.

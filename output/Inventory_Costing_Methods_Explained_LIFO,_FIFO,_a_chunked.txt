The Three Main Inventory Costing Methods There
are three primary costing methods used for periodic and perpetual inventory systems: 1.
==================================================

FIFO: First-In, First-Out The FIFO inventory valuation method assumes that the oldest
inventory units purchased are sold first.
==================================================

Inventory value reflects the costs of the most
recent purchases.
==================================================
Chunk 14:
Cost of goods sold is calculated using costs of the oldest units.

Chunk 15:
Results in higher net income and higher inventory asset value.

2.

Inventory

Example LIFO Inventory Costing Problem Let’s apply
the LIFO method to solve an inventory costing problem. Rana Furniture Factory had the
following lumber inventory transactions in June: June 1: Beginning inventory of 5 units at
$16 per unit June 5: Purchased 13 units at $20 per unit June 19: Sold 9 units June 23:
Purchased 8 units at $18 per unit June 30: Sold 12 units Determine the ending cost of
inventory on June 30 using the LIFO perpetual inventory method. Solution: Beginning
inventory on June 1 = 5 units at $16 per unit = $80 Purchase on June 5 of 13 units at $20
per unit = $260 Sale on June 19 of 9 units removed from latest purchase: 9 units removed
at $20 per unit = $180 Remaining inventory is 13–9 = 4 units at $20 per unit = $80
Purchase on June 23 of 8 units at $18 per unit = $144 Total inventory = 4 units at $20 per
unit + 8 units at $18 per unit = $80 + $144 = $224 Sale on June 30 of 12 units removed the
latest 8 units at $18 and 4 units at $20 8 units removed at $18 per unit = $144 4 units
removed at $20 per unit = $80 Remaining inventory is 0 units Ending inventory on June 30
is 0 units Using the LIFO perpetual method demonstrates the flow of inventory costs and
allows us to arrive at the accurate ending inventory valuation.

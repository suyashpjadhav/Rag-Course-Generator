Chunk 16:
When you make a digital purchase, a
smooth process ensures the funds securely move from your account to the merchant’s. Here’s
a simplified breakdown of the five key steps in this payment processing flow: Step 1:
Initiating the Payment The customer interacts with the merchant’s point-of-sale (POS)
system.
==================================================
Chunk 17:
This could involve swiping a card, using contactless payment (like a mobile
wallet), or entering card details on an online checkout. The POS system then sends the
payment amount and card information securely to the acquiring bank via a payment gateway.
==================================================
Chunk 18:
Step 2: Reaching Out for Approval The acquiring bank, representing the merchant, receives
the payment details and reaches out to the customer’s issuing bank through the appropriate
card network. This step essentially asks the issuing bank for permission to complete the
transaction.
==================================================
Chunk 19:
Step 3: Verification and Authorization The issuing bank, representing the
customer, verifies the received information — card number, CVV code, expiration date — and
checks if sufficient funds are available. If everything is valid, the issuing bank sends
an authorization code back to the acquiring bank using the same card network.
==================================================
Chunk 20:
Step 4: Fund
Authorization and Reserve Upon transaction approval, the issuing bank sets aside the
purchase amount from the customer’s account. This signifies the customer’s go-ahead for
the payment, although the funds haven’t moved to the merchant’s account yet.
==================================================
Chunk 21:
Step 5:
Batching and Settlement Approved transactions are grouped into batches. The merchant
submits the daily batch of transactions with their authorization codes to the acquiring
bank or payment processor.
==================================================
Chunk 22:
The acquirer then requests the authorized amounts from the
issuing bank, triggering the transfer of funds from customers’ accounts to the merchant’s
account. Payment processing fees are also deducted during this stage.

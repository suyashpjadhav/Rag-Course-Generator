What is a
Purchase Order Cycle? A purchase order cycleis a sequential process involved in verifying
and processing a purchase order.
==================================================
Chunk 11:
In the purchase cycle, the first step is the purchase
order process, followed by a budget check, negotiation, and closure.
==================================================
Chunk 12:
Managers can use
purchase orders to track orders, delivery instructions, expenses, and other relevant
information.Streamlining your procurement cyclecan help minimize your business expenses.
==================================================

Steps Involved in the Purchase Order Cycle Let’s discuss the six major steps involved in
the procurement cycle: 1.
==================================================
Chunk 14:
Purchase Order Creation A purchase order is placed only after
the purchase request is approved.
==================================================
Chunk 15:
This is where you decide what will be purchased, the
requisition’s priority level, your spending plan, when you need the product, and who will
be the vendors.
==================================================
Chunk 16:
But what if there are multiple products in the pipeline?
==================================================
Chunk 17:
Then you must
create individual purchase orders for all the items you need.
==================================================
Chunk 18:
Anyone in the organization
can raise purchase requests, but only the procurement team is authorized to place the
purchase order to avoid inconsistency.
==================================================
Chunk 19:
2.
==================================================
Chunk 20:
Budget Check and Approval Once the purchase
order is created, the next step is to analyze the required budget.
==================================================
Chunk 21:
The finance team
performs this check by considering the recent and existing purchase orders.
==================================================
Chunk 22:
If your
organization has an existing contract with a vendor, you can proceed with them first.
==================================================
Chunk 23:
If
an organization doesn’t have an existing vendor, it must look for potential vendors and
submit a request for proposal (RFP) for the required purchase order.
==================================================
Chunk 24:
3.
==================================================
Chunk 25:
Vendor Selection
Selecting a vendoris similar to choosing a trustable business partner.

Chunk 26:
You must check all
boxes in your vendor selection criteria.

Chunk 27:
Your team should be meticulous during vendor
selection.

Chunk 28:
Here are a few vendor selection criteria to help you choose the right vendor
for your organization: Have a clear understanding of your requirements Perform a thorough
quality check before selecting a vendor Run a complete background check on the vendor See
if the vendor has robust customer service Ask your close circle about the vendor
Performing all these checks will help you choose the right vendor.

Chunk 29:
Once the vendor issues
their quote against your proposal, ask your procurement team to validate it against your
requirements.

Chunk 30:
Include your stakeholders and partners in this process.
==================================================
Chunk 31:
Stakeholders will
bring certain issues to light that you were unaware of.

partners might have trustworthy vendor sources, which can be helpful in this process.

Chunk 33:
Overall, this increases transparency and enhances relationships.

4.
==================================================
Chunk 35:
Negotiation Once
you’ve selected a suitable vendor, you can start your negotiation process.
==================================================
Chunk 36:
You need to be
meticulous in your negotiation to get the maximum value for the price from the vendor.
==================================================
Chunk 37:
Strong negotiation is key to a long-term business partnership.

Chunk 38:
You can send your purchase
order to your vendor once you are done with your negotiation.

Chunk 39:
The procurement contract

Chunk 40:
5.
==================================================
Chunk 41:
Product Delivery The next step is where the business waits for the vendor to produce,
deliver the product and submit an invoice.
==================================================
Chunk 42:
Usually, the vendor sends the invoice along
with the shipment notice, which includes delivery-related information.
==================================================
Chunk 43:
Once the product is
received, the in-house team performs a detailed quality check to see if the products
delivered match the requirement.
==================================================
Chunk 44:
6.
==================================================
Chunk 45:
Three-Way Matching If the delivery is on time and the
product quality matches the requirement, the buyer willsend goods received note (GRN). This is followed by three-way matching where the GRN, the purchase order, and the vendor’s
invoice are compared to check for inconsistencies.
==================================================
Chunk 46:
A three-way match is a necessary step
that helps eliminate disparities between what the business ordered, obtained, and paid
for.
==================================================
Chunk 47:
7.
==================================================
Chunk 48:
Closure If the three-way matching process doesn’t reveal any discrepancies, the
invoice can be forwarded to the finance department for the final payment.
==================================================
Chunk 49:
Once the payment
is made, the order can be closed and saved for future audits.

What is the Purchase Order
Number?
==================================================
Chunk 51:
A purchase order number (PO number) is a number given to the purchase order, which
the buyer usually defines.
==================================================
Chunk 52:
The buyer and the seller will refer to the purchase order
number throughout the process.
==================================================
Chunk 53:
Buyers issue purchase orders to vendors to confirm their
desire to buy products or services, and the document includes essential details such as
the pricing and quantities desired.
==================================================
Chunk 54:
When a vendor receives and confirms the conditions of
a purchase order, it effectively turns into a legally binding agreement.
==================================================
Chunk 55:
These documents
help a business maintain a detailed report of its procurement process, which in turn helps
in future auditing.
==================================================
Chunk 56:
This is where the PO number plays a vital role.
==================================================
Chunk 57:
It enables
organizations on both sides of a transaction to track and quickly compare purchase orders
and eases the auditing and taxation processes for the audit and finance teams.
==================================================
